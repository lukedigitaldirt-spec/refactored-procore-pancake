# Contracts and Governance — the clauses and cadence that make an SLA real

A scorecard and an SLO are only enforceable if the contract and the governance
model behind them are sound. This reference backs all three tools by covering
the paperwork and the operating rhythm that give the numbers teeth.

## Contract clauses that make or break vendor management
- **Service levels & remedies:** the SLA table, measurement method, the party
  that measures, exclusions (agreed maintenance), and the service-credit tiers.
  A credit schedule the calculator can evaluate must be unambiguous.
- **Right to audit / report:** access to evidence for the SLIs and KPIs you
  score. Without it, the scorecard is the vendor's word.
- **Business continuity & exit:** continuity/DR obligations, data portability,
  transition assistance, and a defined exit runway. Plan the divorce at the
  wedding.
- **Security & data protection:** processing terms, breach notification windows,
  sub-processor approval, and flow-down of obligations to fourth parties.
- **Change & governance:** change-control process, the governance bodies and
  their cadence, escalation paths, and relationship owners on both sides.
- **Liability, indemnity, insurance:** caps, carve-outs, and required cover.
- **Pricing mechanics:** benchmarking, indexation, volume tiers, and
  most-favored-customer or gain-share provisions where relevant.

## Governance tiers (operational / management / strategic)
Run the relationship at three altitudes, each with its own forum and cadence:
1. **Operational** — day-to-day delivery, incidents, the running scorecard.
   Weekly or monthly.
2. **Management** — SLA performance, risk register, change pipeline, commercial
   issues. Monthly or quarterly (the QBR lives here).
3. **Strategic** — roadmap alignment, investment, relationship health, renewal.
   Semi-annual or annual, executive-sponsored.
Match the intensity to the vendor's segment (see the Kraljic reference): a
strategic vendor gets all three tiers; a non-critical one may need only an
annual review.

## From measurement to consequence
Governance closes the loop: SLO breach or a sub-2 scorecard criterion triggers a
corrective-action plan with an owner and a date; repeated failure escalates up
the tiers and, ultimately, into the contract's remedies and exit provisions.
Numbers with no consequence attached train everyone to ignore them.
