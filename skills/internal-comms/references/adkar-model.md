# ADKAR — the individual change model

Prosci's ADKAR model (Jeff Hiatt) is a goal-oriented model for **individual**
change. Its premise: organizational change only happens when the people in it
change, one person at a time, through five sequential building blocks. This is
the logic behind `scripts/adkar_assessment.py`.

## The five building blocks (in order)
1. **Awareness** of the need for change — why now, what is the risk of not
   changing.
2. **Desire** to support and participate — the personal choice, driven by
   "what's in it for me" (WIIFM) and visible sponsorship.
3. **Knowledge** of how to change — the processes, tools, skills, and roles
   required.
4. **Ability** to implement the change — demonstrated performance in the real
   job, not just classroom understanding.
5. **Reinforcement** to sustain the change — rewards, recognition, and measures
   that stop people reverting to the old way.

## Why the order is load-bearing
The blocks are a chain. You cannot build genuine Desire in someone who has no
Awareness of why the change matters; you cannot build Ability before Knowledge.
The **first** block that is weak is the barrier point — the gap that blocks
every block after it. Pouring effort into a later block (more training when the
real gap is Desire) wastes energy and breeds cynicism. Diagnose the barrier,
fix it, then move on.

## Barrier point → action
- **Awareness gap:** communicate the business reason and the risk of standing
  still; repeat it through trusted senders (the employee's own manager, then a
  senior sponsor).
- **Desire gap:** address WIIFM, make sponsorship active and visible, and work
  resistance directly rather than wishing it away.
- **Knowledge gap:** deliver role-specific training on the new way of working.
- **Ability gap:** provide coaching, hands-on practice, tools, and time to
  apply knowledge on the job.
- **Reinforcement gap:** reward and measure the new behavior; remove incentives
  that still pay off the old behavior.

## Sponsorship and the sender
Prosci's research consistently finds the **primary sponsor** and the
**employee's direct manager** are the most important senders of change
messages. Awareness and Desire messages land when they come from these people,
not from a project team or a corporate newsletter alone.

## Using it with the tool
Score each dimension 1-5 from survey, interview, or manager judgment. The tool
returns the overall average, per-dimension pass/fail against a threshold, and
the barrier point with its matched action. Re-score after intervening — the
barrier moves as you clear each block.
